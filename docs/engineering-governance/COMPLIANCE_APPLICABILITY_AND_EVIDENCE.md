# Compliance Applicability and Evidence

## 1. Purpose

Create a defensible process for identifying potentially applicable laws, regulations, contracts, standards, and internal policies, then tracking evidence for relevant requirements.

## 2. No assumed applicability

Applicability depends on facts such as jurisdiction, entity type, industry, data, users, processing purpose, contractual commitments, and system function. Do not infer applicability from a technology stack or a generic checklist.

AI agents may identify questions and summarize authoritative material supplied or verified by qualified people. They must not provide an unsupported legal conclusion.

## 3. Applicability workflow

1. Identify the organization, product, service, users, data, processing, locations, and business relationships.
2. List potentially relevant obligations and their source/version/date.
3. Record an applicability decision: `Applicable`, `Not applicable`, `Potentially applicable`, or `Not assessed`.
4. Record the rationale, assumptions, decision owner, and date.
5. For applicable requirements, map requirement to control, owner, evidence, and review frequency.
6. Obtain qualified legal/compliance advice where interpretation or legal advice is needed.
7. Reassess after material changes or updates to authoritative requirements.

## 4. Evidence quality

Evidence should identify:
- requirement and control;
- artifact or system location;
- evidence owner and collection date;
- scope and period covered;
- method of verification;
- result and limitations;
- reviewer and review date, where required.

Evidence must be authentic, relevant, current enough for the claim, and protected from unauthorized disclosure.

## 5. Claims and reporting

Do not claim certification, compliance, audit success, or legal conformity merely because:
- a policy exists;
- a checklist is complete;
- a control is planned;
- an AI agent says it passed;
- a sample or partial test succeeded.

Use precise language such as `evidence reviewed for the stated scope` and disclose exclusions, gaps, and unverified areas.

## 6. Privacy and confidentiality

Collect the minimum evidence necessary. Redact secrets and personal data. Do not copy credentials, tokens, private keys, or sensitive records into governance reports. Restrict evidence access according to organizational policy.
