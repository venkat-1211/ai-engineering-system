# Continuous Improvement

## Improvement loop

1. Collect signals: incidents, defects, reviews, audits, user feedback, delivery metrics, near misses, and retrospectives.
2. Define the problem using observable evidence.
3. Analyze contributing factors and root causes without defaulting to blame.
4. Prioritize by impact, risk, recurrence, and effort.
5. Assign one accountable owner and measurable completion criteria.
6. Implement corrective or preventive actions.
7. Verify effectiveness after an appropriate period.
8. Update policies, templates, training, or automation when justified.
9. Share relevant learning while protecting confidential information.

## Action record

Record problem, evidence, impact, root-cause hypothesis, action, owner, due date, success measure, verification method, status, and closure decision.

## Avoid ineffective improvement

- Do not close actions solely because a document was edited.
- Do not confuse activity with effectiveness.
- Avoid superficial root-cause labels such as “human error” without examining system conditions.
- Do not create metrics that encourage hiding defects or incidents.
- Reopen actions when effectiveness is not demonstrated.

## Retrospectives

Use blameless, evidence-based discussion. Capture what worked, what did not, unexpected outcomes, and specific actions. Respect confidentiality and applicable HR processes.
