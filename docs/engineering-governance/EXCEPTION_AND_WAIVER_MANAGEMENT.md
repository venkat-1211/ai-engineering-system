# Exception and Waiver Management

## Purpose

Control deviations from an approved policy or standard without normalizing permanent bypasses.

## Required exception record

- unique ID;
- policy/control and exact requirement;
- project/system and affected scope;
- reason and alternatives considered;
- risk assessment and residual risk;
- compensating controls;
- accountable owner and implementation owner;
- authorized approver;
- start date and expiry date;
- monitoring and review cadence;
- remediation plan and target date;
- status and closure evidence.

## Rules

1. No exception is valid without required human approval.
2. AI agents may prepare requests but cannot approve them.
3. Scope must be narrow and explicit.
4. Exceptions must be time-bound; no silent or indefinite waivers.
5. Higher-risk exceptions require appropriate independent review.
6. Expired exceptions must be treated as expired, not implicitly renewed.
7. Renewal requires fresh justification, risk review, and approval.
8. Record compensating controls and verify their operation.
9. Close only when the requirement is met, the exception is formally retired, or an authorized decision documents the outcome.

## Emergency handling

Follow the organization's incident and emergency authority process. Record the decision as soon as practicable, including who authorized it and what retrospective review is required. Do not use emergency language to bypass normal controls without authority.
